# Abstract

Cross-sectional stock-return prediction with neural networks almost always optimises Mean Squared Error (MSE) or a close relative, yet the downstream decision — a long-short portfolio — depends on the cross-sectional rank of the predictions, not on their calibrated magnitudes. Under this mismatch, a quadratic loss gives disproportionate training weight to heavy-tailed outliers, and the resulting model trades ranking quality for calibration quality that the portfolio does not use. This report asks whether a hybrid loss that combines a directional-accuracy term with a robust magnitude term can outperform both traditional regression losses and pure directional losses under a controlled evaluation protocol.

The study uses a CRSP-style US equity monthly panel with a static protocol: training window `1990-01` to `1994-12` (60 monthly cross-sections) and main out-of-sample test window `1995-01` to `1996-12` (24 monthly cross-sections). The model is a fixed multi-layer perceptron (MLP) with hidden widths `[64, 32, 16]`, ReLU activations, and dropout $0.2$, consuming a 15-dimensional cumulative-return and cumulative-turnover feature vector. Training uses batch size $1024$ and 20 epochs of Adam; the trained model is then evaluated month-by-month on a top/bottom-10% signal-tilted long-short portfolio with a 5% per-name cap. Performance is reported as annualised Sharpe ($\sqrt{12}$-scaled monthly mean/std), cumulative 24-month return, and, for multi-seed phases, the coefficient of variation $\mathrm{CV} = \sigma_S / |\mu_S|$ across three random seeds.

Four experimental phases are run under identical data, features, architecture, and portfolio settings. A single-seed baseline compares seven loss families (MSE, MedSE, MADL, GMADL, IMADL, and two hybrid multiplicative variants); a Phase 1.5 sweep scans nine parameterised hybrid variants (five additive A-series and four multiplicative M-series); Phase 2 refines the M2-robust loss with a γ parameter and evaluates five γ settings across three seeds; an integrated Phase 2 summary extends the comparison to IMADL-m2 α, IMADL-GMADL β, and adaptive-λ sweeps under the same multi-seed protocol; and a Phase 2.2-fix1 probe tests loss-component normalisation on the top three candidates.

The multi-seed evidence identifies `m2_robust_gamma07` (multiplicative hybrid with robustness parameter $\gamma = 0.07$) as the best-supported choice, with mean Sharpe $0.9156$, CV $0.1808$, and mean cumulative return $+27.99\%$ across three seeds; no seed produces a negative Sharpe in this configuration, and the result is approximately flat under loss-component normalisation (normalised mean Sharpe $0.9112$). The high-return alternative `m2_robust_gamma10` reaches a mean Sharpe of $1.0043$ but has CV $0.5613$ — three times that of γ07 — and degrades to mean Sharpe $0.4072$ under normalisation; it is only appropriate where operators knowingly accept higher seed-sensitivity. The stable fallback `imadl_m2_alpha06`, drawn from an independent parameterisation, achieves mean Sharpe $0.6895$ with CV $0.2443$ and the largest mean cumulative return of the three candidates at $+30.42\%$. Traditional regression losses (MSE, MedSE) and pure directional losses (MADL, GMADL, IMADL) do not produce robust positive Sharpes at the same single seed, and the absolute-loss family additionally exhibits very negative point-prediction R² values while retaining competitive ranking performance — a dissociation between calibration and portfolio quality that the report makes explicit.

Three boundaries constrain the conclusions. First, the evaluation uses a single static 24-month window; the rolling-window extension is listed as future work. Second, multi-seed evaluations use three seeds, which is sufficient to differentiate CV at the order-of-magnitude level but not to bound CV to the second decimal. Third, the loss-component normalisation probe's scale ratios are diagnostics-estimated rather than measured by a per-component logger, which is flagged as a priority instrumentation task. Within these boundaries the report provides a verifiable recommendation — `m2_robust_gamma07` as the primary loss, with explicit high-return and stable-fallback alternatives — together with a source-of-truth evidence map that ties every reported number to a named artifact file.

**Keywords.** loss function design; robust regression; MADL; GMADL; cross-sectional stock-return prediction; long-short portfolio; multi-layer perceptron; multi-seed robustness; coefficient of variation; CRSP.
